AI Receptionist Upsell

Four emails to existing SoundCurve customers. Days 0, 5, 10 and 16. Drafts for review.

Changed in this pass: the deadline is gone. No countdown, no end date, nothing to enforce. The offer is a standing perk for existing customers. The opening line no longer names trades, so food service and legal see themselves in it. The California disclosure claim is now stated as policy. Sender is now plain SoundCurve, not SoundCurve Sales: this list filters on sender, and “Sales” gets deleted before the subject is read. Subject lines are deliberately plain rather than clever, because brand recognition already earns the open and a teaser reads as marketing filler on top of it. None of them can now be mistaken for an outage or a cancellation notice, which two of the old ones were. New email 3 sells what comes back after the call: the write-up, and the read on how the caller sounded. It pays off email 1’s premise, since email 1 says you missed the call because you were busy and this says here is what you missed and whether it mattered. Email 1 now sells the intent routing: it is the menu that understands what the caller wants and sends them to the right person. Email 2 now carries the customisation proof and sets expectations honestly: the demo runs on a sample business, so the reader is told to judge the conversation rather than the answers. Emails 2 and 3 reference what came before them. All four buttons go to the same customer-only landing page.
